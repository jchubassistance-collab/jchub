You are asked for “a single PDF file” and you have ten photos taken with your phone: both sides of an ID card, statements, receipts, handwritten homework. Sending ten attachments is the best way to have a page go missing. The fix: gather them into one PDF, one image per page, in the right order.

With JcHub’s [Images to PDF](/en/tools/images-to-pdf) tool, it takes less than a minute, with nothing to install.

## What you need

- Images in **JPG, JPEG or PNG** format. Those are the formats of phone photos and screenshots.
- A browser, on a computer or a phone.

That’s it. No account to create, no software to download.

## The steps, one by one

1. **Open the** [Images to PDF](/en/tools/images-to-pdf) **tool** and add your images.
2. **Put them in the right order.** Each image becomes a page, in the order of the list. Use the arrows to move an image up or down: front before back, page 1 before page 2.
3. **Pick the page size.** Three choices: **A4**, the standard for official documents in most of the world; **US Letter**, if your recipient is in North America; or **fit to each image**, to keep the exact proportions of every photo.
4. **Set the orientation**, portrait or landscape, and the **margin**: none, small or large. A small margin looks cleaner when printed.
5. **Adjust the placement.** “Show everything” displays the whole image without cutting it. “Fill the page” covers the whole sheet, even if the edges get cropped a little.
6. **Fix an image if needed.** You can rotate it if the photo is sideways, move it on the page by dragging it, and apply the same setting to every page.
7. **Click “Convert to PDF”** and download your file.

Thirty seconds, as promised, once your photos are ready.

## Take good photos before converting

The PDF will never be easier to read than your photos. A few habits make all the difference:

- **Light, but no flash.** Daylight near a window avoids glare on glossy paper.
- **The document flat, shot straight on.** A document photographed at an angle gives slanted lines that are hard to read.
- **A plain, dark background** under a white sheet, so the edges stand out.
- **Check sharpness** by zooming in before moving on. One blurry figure on a statement is enough to have a file sent back.

If an image has too much around it, crop it first with the [social image resizer and cropper](/en/tools/social-image-resizer-cropper).

## When this habit pays off

- **Job or school applications**: diplomas, transcripts and ID, gathered in the requested order.
- **Expense reports**: all the month’s receipts in one file for accounting.
- **Homework and handwritten papers**: every page of an assignment in a single upload.
- **Online paperwork**: many portals accept only one file per requested document.

## What if the PDF is too heavy?

Recent phone photos often weigh several megabytes each. Ten of them can produce a PDF too heavy for a form limited to 2 MB or for an email. In that case, run it through the [Compress PDF](/en/tools/compress-pdf) tool, which cuts the size sharply while keeping the text readable.

Already have a PDF and want to add your photos to it? Convert the images, then join both files with the [Merge and split PDF](/en/tools/merge-split-pdf) tool.

Need the text from a photo, to copy or edit it? The [image to text (OCR)](/en/tools/image-to-text-ocr) tool extracts it for you.

## What happens to your images

The conversion runs on our servers. Your images are kept in private, temporary storage while the PDF is created, then deleted. The tool is free, up to **5 conversions a day** per IP address, which is plenty for one application.

## Key takeaways

- One image becomes one page, in the order of your list: sort them before converting.
- Choose **A4** for official documents, with a small margin for printing.
- Take care of your photos: natural light, document flat, sharp image.
- If the final file is too heavy, compress it before sending.
