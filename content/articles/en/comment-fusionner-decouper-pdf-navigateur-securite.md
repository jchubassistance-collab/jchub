# How to securely merge and split your PDFs in the browser

On a daily basis, handling PDF documents has become essential. Whether you are putting together an administrative file, gathering supporting documents for a bank loan, assembling invoices for accounting, or extracting a chapter from a university course, we constantly need to modify the structure of our PDF files.

Yet, this seemingly simple task often poses two major issues: the heaviness of traditional desktop software and the privacy risks associated with using standard online conversion services.

---

## The hidden risks of traditional online PDF converters

When you search for "merge PDF" on a search engine, the majority of free tools ask you to upload your files to remote servers. The document is sent, processed on a third-party server, and then downloaded back to your machine.

This method has serious drawbacks:

- **Sensitive data leaks**: Your invoices, ID cards, employment contracts, or pay stubs pass through infrastructures whose location and retention policy you do not control.
- **Slow process**: Sending large files (several tens of megabytes) directly depends on your upload internet speed.
- **Size and quota limits**: Most platforms restrict the number of documents or impose waiting times unless you subscribe to a paid plan.

---

## The modern solution: 100% local processing in the browser

Thanks to recent developments in web technologies, it is no longer necessary to send your documents to a remote server to perform file manipulations. Modern JavaScript engines directly execute PDF reading and reconstruction algorithms on your computer's or phone's CPU.

This is exactly how the [Fusion et découpe de PDF](https://jchub.dev/fr/outils/fusionner-decouper-pdf) tool available on JcHub works. Your files remain permanently in your device's memory. No document ever leaves your computer.

---

## Practical use cases: When should you merge and split?

### 1. Preparing an application or rental file
Landlords and real estate agencies frequently request a single file containing your entire ID, proof of address, and pay stubs. Instead of sending 10 scattered attachments, you can combine all your supporting documents into a single, structured document.

### 2. Extracting accounting documents
If you receive a 50-page monthly activity report but your accountant only cares about the summary invoice on page 3, splitting the file saves you from storing and sharing unnecessary volume.

### 3. Assembling quotes and technical appendices
Contractors and freelancers often need to combine a commercial proposal written in Word and technical schematics produced in other formats. Once each item is exported as a PDF, merging them gives you a clean and consistent final commercial proposal.

## Tips to keep your PDFs well organized

- **Clean up before assembling**: Delete blank or unnecessary pages before merging your documents to reduce the final file size.
- **Name your files clearly**: Use an explicit naming convention like `2026_Devis_Projet_Client.pdf` rather than `Document1.pdf`.
- **Check the final size**: If your final PDF exceeds the maximum size allowed by your administrative forms or emails, feel free to use a suitable compression tool as a complement.

Opting for web tools that run locally guarantees maximum speed while fully protecting all your personal information.
