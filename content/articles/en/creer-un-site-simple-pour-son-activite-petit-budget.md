“I need a website, but I don’t have the budget.” It’s the sentence web designers hear most often. Yet for most small businesses, a simple website is plenty: a few clear pages saying what you do, where to find you and how to reach you. Here’s how to get one without breaking the bank, and without picking the wrong solution.

## First: do you really need a website?

To get started, a free **Google Business Profile** and a well-filled **WhatsApp Business** already cover the essentials: being found and being contacted. Our article on [free tools to launch your business online](/en/blog/free-tools-to-launch-your-small-online-business) details those first steps.

A website becomes useful when you want to:

- **look more professional** to companies or public bodies;
- **present your offer in detail**: services, prices, past work;
- **show up on Google** for specific searches;
- **receive requests** or bookings online.

## The four options, from cheapest to most complete

**1. An online website builder.** Platforms let you build a site from templates, without coding. Fast and often available with a free plan, but limited: an address like `yourshop.platform.com` and the platform’s ads. Paid plans remove those limits, for a monthly subscription.

**2. WordPress with hosting.** You rent hosting and install WordPress, which is free, with a theme. More freedom, but it takes some time to learn, and regular updates to stay secure.

**3. A custom website by a professional.** Designed for your business, fast, in your image. It’s the most expensive option upfront, but often the most durable, especially if you need specific features: booking, mobile money payment, customer accounts.

**4. A one-page website.** Often overlooked, it’s sometimes the best choice: a single well-built page with your offer, your photos, your address and a WhatsApp button. Cheap, quick to create, quick to load.

## The pages you can’t skip

Even simple, your website must answer four questions:

1. **Who are you and what do you do?** One clear sentence at the top of the home page.
2. **What do you offer?** Your products or services, with photos and, if possible, prices or a range.
3. **Why trust you?** Customer reviews, past work, years of experience.
4. **How to reach you?** Phone, WhatsApp, address, opening hours, and a map.

Add a **legal notice** where your country requires one, and a **privacy policy** if you have a form.

## The domain name: your address on the Internet

A domain in your name (`yourshop.com` or your country’s extension) instantly looks more professional than a platform address. It’s rented yearly, usually between $10 and $20 for a `.com` depending on the registrar. Make sure it’s **registered in your name**, not your provider’s: it’s your address, it must belong to you.

## The steps to launch your website

1. **Write down what you want**: goals, pages, features, budget. Our [website requirements template](/en/resources/website-requirements-template) helps you do it in an hour.
2. **Prepare your content**: texts, photos, logo. It’s the most common cause of delays.
3. **Choose your solution** among the four options, based on your budget and time.
4. **If you hire a provider**, sign a clear contract: price, deadlines, ownership of the site. Our [website development contract template](/en/resources/website-development-contract-template) is suited to OHADA member countries.
5. **Before going live**, check everything with our [website launch checklist](/en/resources/website-launch-checklist).

## Costly traps

- **A heavy, slow site**: your customers are often on phones, on 3G or 4G. Heavy images drive them away.
- **A site never updated**: wrong hours, changed number. Better three up-to-date pages than twenty abandoned ones.
- **Not having the access**: always ask for the logins to the hosting, the domain name and the site’s admin.
- **Wanting everything from day one**: start simple, add the shop or booking when the need is real.
